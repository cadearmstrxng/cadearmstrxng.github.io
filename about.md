## Bio

  <img src="/assets/img/headshot.jpg"
       alt="Photo of Cade Armstrong"
       class="about-photo-float">

I’m Cade Armstrong, a first-year doctoral student in Aerospace Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin, advised by <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/lukethomaspeterson" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Dr. Luke Peterson</strong></a> and <a href="https://www.ae.utexas.edu/people/faculty/faculty-directory/topcu" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Dr. Ufuk Topcu</strong></a>. My primary research interests include orbital mechanics, trajectory design and optimization, multi-agent systems, and game theory. Through my research, I aim to improve the efficiency of space missions via improved decision-making and optimized trajectories. Additionally, I am passionate about teaching and aspire to teach at the university level, mentoring future engineers.

<hr class="bio-divider">

I spent the majority of my undergraduate studies conducting research with <a href="https://www.ae.utexas.edu/people/faculty/faculty-directory/fridovich-keil" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Dr. David Fridovich-Keil</a> and the <a href="https://clearoboticslab.github.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Control and Learning for Autonomous Robotics (CLeAR) Lab</a> at UT Austin. Primarily focused on multi-agent interactions, I learned the fundamentals of multi-agent control and optimization through the lense of dynamic game theory. During this time, I worked alongside <a href="https://ryanjpark03.github.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ryan J Park</a> to develop a novel game formulation that models foresighted agent behavior and an algorithm for inferring agents' level of foresight from online data.

After recieving my B.S. in Aerospace Engineering from UT Austin, I began working the <a href="https://csr.utexas.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Center for Space Research</a>, where I worked on problems pertaining to the orbital analysis, estimation, and prediction of the GRACE-FO formation. Additionally, I created a simulation for GRACE-FO's inter-satellite range readings and implemented error reduction methods for the subsequent data processing.


## Interests
Outside of academics, I enjoy reading sci-fi fantasy books, listening to and discovering new music, playing soccer, and trying new restaurants. I try to stay curious about the world around me and enjoy learning about new topics!
