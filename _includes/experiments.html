Experiment 1: Crosswalk Simulation

This experiment involves $N = 2$ pedestrians navigating a crosswalk at an intersection on the UT Austin campus. The interesection is unique in that it becomes safe for all pedestrian crossing simultaneously, allowing students to cross diagonally and interact with one another.

Experimental Setup

We simulate two short-sighted ($\gamma < 1$) delivery robots who begin at the top right and left corners, respectively, and wish to reach their goals at the corner's diagonal to them while minimizing their control effort. As such, we can model robot $i$'s cost as: $$ \begin{align} J_{\text{robot}}^i = \sum_{t=0}^T (\gamma^i)^t [w_\text{goal}^i ||p_t^i - \theta_\text{goal}^i||_2^2 + w_\text{ctrl}^i ||u_t^i||_2^2]. \end{align} $$ where $p_t^i$ is agent $i$'s position at time $t$, $w_\text{goal}^i$ and $w_\text{ctrl}^i$ are cost weight terms for the goal and control cost, and $\theta_\text{goal}^i$ are parameters that define agent $i$'s goal position.

We model the robots as point masses with bounds on the velocity and control inputs, assume a time discretization of $\Delta = 0.1$, and play a game over $T = 25$ time steps. Each agent's dynamics are modeled with a standard double integrator system, with the game state $x_t = [x_t^{1,\top},x_t^{1,\top}]^\top$. We additionally include collision avoidance inequality constraints to each agent such that the agents stay a certain distance $d_\text{min}$ away from each other.
